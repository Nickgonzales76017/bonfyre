Bonfÿre is a typed operating machine for turning intent into authorized work, effects, evidence, and learning. Aurekai is its evidence-bound learning loop. Neither is a generic agent wrapper.

That distinction matters because a useful machine has to preserve more than a prompt and a tool call. It must know who or what is acting, which capability is needed, what authority applies, which effect is being attempted, what resource and cost constraints are active, and what evidence would count as completion.

The path from intent to evidence

The governing path is deliberately explicit:

  1. Identity, Authority, and CapabilityClosure determine the actor, permission boundary, and gap between a named capability and a workload-proven realization.
  2. WorkGraph owns durable missions, dependencies, leases, cooling, and completion contracts.
  3. ProviderGraph, context, resources, and MoneyGraph constrain how work may be realized. A native command, a local model, an agent carrier, an external provider, and human work remain different choices.
  4. EffectKernel and AgentSession execute within authority and idempotency ceilings.
  5. ReceiptEnvelope, WitnessDAG, and EvidenceGraph record what happened and how it supports—or contradicts—a claim.
  6. Aurekai may discover candidate patterns from those traces, but promotion still requires replay, backtesting, gyms, shadow execution, and proof.

This keeps three common shortcuts from becoming false claims. Reachability is not authority. A subprocess exit code is not semantic completion. A repeated pattern is not a proven primitive.

Agents are carriers, not semantic owners

Codex, Claude, local models, and distributed workers can all realize bounded work. They do not replace Bonfÿre’s named powers, authority graph, work ontology, receipt contracts, institutional lineages, or evidence system. A carrier can change while the meaning of the mission remains stable.

The same rule applies to integrations. External tools keep their native identity and lifecycle behind a ForeignTwin and effect boundary. Connecting a CMS, repository, provider, or social surface does not grant authority over the remote institution.

Architecture claims must carry maturity

Bonfÿre’s Architecture Atlas records each organ with an explicit maturity level: architectural, declared, implemented, measured, or proven. Measured and proven entries require witnesses. Simplified diagrams are reversible views over the registry, not replacements for it.

The frozen Generation-10/V8.1 reference passed 1,654 direct executable checks and 295 structural lineage checks, while the live repository remains intentionally honest about mixed maturity. WorkGraph, occurrence, capability composition, authority, and institutional grammars have executable witnesses; the full Aurekai trace-to-promotion loop remains incomplete and cannot be claimed as proven.

That is the larger aim: an operating machine that can move quickly without erasing the difference between a name, an implementation, a measurement, a proof, and an authorized external effect.
