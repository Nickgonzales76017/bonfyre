Business software

From a viral X post

The new accounting app is real. The books still have to add up.

A November 2026 post on X describes a parent replacing QuickBooks with something made using Codex. It captures a major change: people with modest programming experience can now build tools specifically for themselves.

That does not tell us whether the tool has reconciled a bank account, handled a reversal, preserved a record after a software update, or produced a report an accountant can rely on. Those jobs have different requirements from entering an invoice on a clean screen. It is possible to believe in the breakthrough while asking those questions.

THE CLAIM

A business owner may be able to build an application instead of buying one.

From public records to a usable collection

A city council meeting should be easier to find again.

Meeting agendas, video, attachments and decisions are often spread across different systems. For a resident trying to understand a local issue, the challenge isn't a lack of information so much as knowing which record belongs with which discussion.

Our Dallas Council research has explored exact file-number joins, archived video chapters and the differences between indexed and available records. A confident story has to preserve those differences. A video mentioning a proposal is not a vote approving it; an automatically matched title is not a verified record.

Trade and inventory

Next / Creators ↓

From a listing to a real room

The price is on the listing. The work is everywhere else.

A liquidation lot, a certified coin or a refurbished instrument comes with descriptions that look comparable until someone tries to buy, ship, inspect or resell the thing.

GSA Auctions shows how public surplus can be offered through structured listings, but each sale still has its terms and condition. NGC's guarantee gives an example of item-specific third-party claims that a marketplace should not silently substitute with a generic condition badge. A product's actual identity matters more than another listing card.

This is one reason we connect catalogue identities, procurement, room planning and seller information. A person considering an object needs to know what it is, what is claimed about it, and where it can be used. A room scene is useful when it retains the actual selected product.

From a recording to a body of work

A good interview deserves somewhere to live.

A creator might publish a clip, the full recording, a transcript, a newsletter and a paid companion resource. Those should not become five unrelated copies of the same conversation.

The RSS specification remains useful for distributing new publications. It doesn't by itself preserve the people involved, licences, media sources or collection structure. That work belongs in the publisher's own archive and profile.

The creator experience we want is not an AI-generated feed of endless short clips. It's a coherent record of a person's output that can support writing, sound, community and business services while keeping the source discoverable.

From a post about coding to a release

Hundreds of pull requests. What did the customer get?

Boris Cherny's December 2025 post described remarkable Claude Code output. Karpathy's original vibe-coding post helped name an important new style of building. The excitement is justified. What gets lost in many retellings is that code creation, integration and dependable service are different parts of the job.

The 2025 METR developer experiment showed a slowdown for experienced contributors under its particular conditions. Its 2026 update described evidence of improvement and why task selection made newer comparisons difficult. There is no honest universal multiplier for all programming tasks.
