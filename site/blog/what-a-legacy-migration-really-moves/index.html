A website migration moves a business, not just files

Why old WordPress, PHP and hosting migrations are opportunities to improve operations while keeping history intact.

Aurekai / BonfÿreNovember 7, 2026Edited field notes

An old hosting server might have thirty websites on it. Some run WordPress versions that haven't been touched in years. Some depend on old PHP behavior. Moving them to a modern system sounds straightforward until the customer opens a form, downloads a report or finds that a plugin no longer works.

We spent time on this question with a portfolio running on an aging InterWorx installation. Upgrading the hosting control panel meant changing the operating system. Changing the operating system meant changing what the sites could run. The real work was finding out which business processes the sites still supported.

Start with what people use

Before moving anything, look at the forms, reports, database tables, scheduled jobs and content. A customer may not care about the WordPress version, but they care deeply about whether orders arrive and staff can find the data they need.

There are several reasonable destinations. Some sites should remain WordPress on healthier hosting. Others may be better served by a portable content system or a new front end over the existing business data. An agency might manage the migration and ongoing maintenance across multiple customers.

Use the move to understand the business

This is where Aurekai's CMS, native application work and external database connections become useful. We can inspect a site's existing content and behavior, keep a relationship to the original records, and build a customer-facing experience that fits the business. The migration may also reveal a missing CRM, reporting or finance workflow worth improving.

W3Techs' WordPress usage survey is one reminder of how much business still runs on established website software. The Aurekai product guide connects modernization to the broader shared operating model.

A successful migration doesn't need to turn every customer into a different version of the same app. It needs to make their existing business more dependable, and leave them with clearer choices about what to improve next.
