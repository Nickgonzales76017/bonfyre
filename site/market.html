The market

Five areas we're following closely.

These are public market indicators and original-source observations. They help us choose where to invest engineering time. They aren't estimates of Aurekai's customers, revenue or market share.

Small businesses use more software than ever.

The U.S. Chamber's October 2026 small-business report found that 41% of surveyed small businesses use at least six technology platforms, and 66% use AI. A shop may have different systems for selling, accounts, email, marketing, stock and staff. Aurekai's CRM, Frappe and account-application work addresses the practical job of making those systems useful together.

Read U.S. Chamber's 2026 report ↗

The existing website still matters.

W3Techs' November 7, 2026 survey puts WordPress on 39.4% of measured websites. Many businesses have invested years in sites built around PHP, MySQL, plugins, content and customer workflows. A migration can be a chance to modernize the hosting and business operations, but the orders, reports and information already there need to keep working. That's why our work includes old-site migrations and database integration.

Read W3Techs website survey ↗

Creators need more than a publishing button.

The IAB's September 2026 CreatorFronts recap describes U.S. creator advertising as a market of roughly $44 billion in projected 2026 spend. It points to the need for practical infrastructure around creators. Aurekai Live, channels, source libraries, audio tools, profiles and products are ways for a creator to organize a body of work and the business around it.

Read IAB's 2026 CreatorFronts recap ↗

AI is widespread. Connected agents are earlier.

According to the Stanford 2026 AI Index, 88% of organizations surveyed reported using AI in at least one function in 2025. Agent deployments were still in the single digits across nearly all surveyed business functions. Bonfÿre is being developed to connect automated tasks with real business records, applications and account permissions.

Read Stanford's 2026 AI Index ↗

A product has more than one useful address.

The GS1 Digital Link standard shows how a product identifier can connect to different online information: details, traceability or other related resources. We see the same need across supplier catalogues, procurement, product options, saved room plans and shopping. A product needs a stable identity even when each application shows something different about it.
