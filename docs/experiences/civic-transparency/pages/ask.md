# Ask your city

Any Texas resident can ask a city for its plate reader records in writing, and the city has to answer within set deadlines. This page has the letter, the list of things worth asking for, and what happens after you send it.

## What to ask for

Ask for records that already exist. A city does not have to make new ones, so name documents and counts it will already have.

1. The contract with the camera company, every amendment, and the invoices paid in the last fiscal year.
2. How many readers the city runs or pays for, and how many cameras on private property (shopping centers, neighborhood associations) share their reads with the police. Ask for the counts only.
3. The written policy for the system: who may search, what reason they must give, and how long reads are kept.
4. The list of other agencies that can search the city's reads, and the list of networks the city's officers can search.
5. A count of searches by month for the last twelve months, split into searches by the city's own officers and searches by other agencies.
6. Any audit, compliance review or misuse investigation of the system, with its outcome.

## The letter

Send it to the city's public information officer. Most cities list an email address for requests on their website, often under "open records" or "public information". Keep the date you sent it.

```
To the Officer for Public Information, City of ______

Under the Texas Public Information Act (Texas Government Code, Chapter 552),
I request copies of the following records about the city's automated
license plate reader system:

1. Contracts, amendments and statements of work with the vendor, and
   invoices paid in fiscal year ____.
2. The number of license plate readers the city operates or funds, and the
   number of privately owned cameras whose reads the police department can
   search. Counts only; I am not asking for locations.
3. The written policy, general order or standard operating procedure that
   governs use of the system, including data retention.
4. The current list of outside agencies with access to the city's reads, and
   of outside networks the department can search.
5. For each of the last twelve months, the number of searches run by city
   personnel and the number run by outside agencies. If only the search log
   exists, please release it with license plate numbers and details of open
   investigations redacted.
6. Any audit, compliance review or misuse investigation of the system
   completed since ____, and its outcome.

I prefer electronic copies. If the cost will exceed $40, please send an
itemized estimate first.

Name: ______
Email or mailing address: ______
```

## What happens next

- The city must produce the records promptly. If it cannot within ten business days, it has to tell you in writing when they will be ready.
- If the city wants to hold something back, it has ten business days to ask the Texas Attorney General for a ruling, and it must send you a copy of that request. You can write to the Attorney General with your side.
- Cities often cite the law enforcement exception (section 552.108) for details tied to investigations. Counts, contracts and policies are usually released.
- Copies can carry a charge. Asking for electronic copies and for an estimate first keeps it small.

## Questions for a council meeting

Public comment usually allows about three minutes. One clear question gets a better answer than six.

- How many plate readers does the city pay for, and how many private cameras feed into the same system?
- How long are reads of cars that are not wanted for anything kept?
- Which outside agencies can search our residents' reads, and who approved each one?
- How many searches were run last month, and how many of them gave a case number?
- When was the system last audited, and where can residents read the result?
