# Reading a search log

Every time an officer searches a plate reader system, the system writes a line in a log. Some cities publish summaries of that log, and anyone can ask for it under the Public Information Act. This page explains what a line holds, what a city usually redacts, and what a redacted log can still tell you.

## What one line records

Logs differ between companies, but most lines carry the same few things. Some cities withhold the name of the officer who searched. Reasons usually come out with case details removed, case numbers for open investigations are usually held back, and plate numbers are withheld.

| Part | What it is | Released? |
|---|---|---|
| Time of the search | When the officer ran it | Yes |
| Agency | Which department the officer works for | Yes |
| Account | The officer or user who ran it | Sometimes |
| Reason | Free text the officer typed, such as "stolen vehicle" | Mostly |
| Case number | The incident the search belongs to | Often not |
| What was searched | A plate number or a description of a car | No |
| Networks searched | Whose cameras the search reached | Yes |
| Time window | How far back the search looked | Yes |

## What you can learn from a redacted log

A log with plates and case details blacked out can still answer the questions councils care about.

- How many searches ran each month, and whether that number is growing.
- How many searches gave a one-word reason or no reason at all.
- How many searches came from agencies outside the city, and which ones.
- Whether searches reach further back than the city's own retention policy says reads are kept.

Count these by month and by agency. A count says the same thing a full log would about how the system is used, without anyone's car in it.

## How this site handles a log

If you send us a log a city released to you, we publish only monthly counts and the names of agencies. We do not post plate numbers, the names of private people, case numbers or anything else that could point to a person.

This site has no tool for checking whether your own car was read, and it will not get one. Answering that question needs the plate log itself, and a plate log is a record of where people drove. If you want to know what a city holds about your vehicle, you can ask the city directly under the same law, using your own plate.

## Where cities publish their own numbers

Some departments post a public page with their policy, their retention period and search counts. If your city has one, it is the first source to read and the first to quote. Send us its address through the [contact page](/contact) and we will link it here with the day we read it.
