Request for Proposals: Website Redesign & Ecosystem Development

CATEGORY: Partnerships

Isabella Senzamici • Oct 6, 2026

Share this story

Digital Strategy, UX/UI Design, CMS Development, Migration, and Ongoing Technical Support

THE OPPORTUNITY AT A GLANCE

Achievement First is seeking an experienced digital agency to architect and build a unified, scalable digital ecosystem. The solution will comprise a main network website, regional hubs, and 41 distinct school-specific websites under a central governance model. 

 

RFP Key Milestones Date / Timeline
RFP Issuance Date October 5, 2026
Vendor Questions Due October 19, 2026
Proposal Submission Deadline November 9, 2026
Finalist Presentations & Demonstrations November 16-20 & November 30-December 4, 2026
Target Selection Notification By December 11, 2026
Project Kickoff January 2027

1. Executive Overview & Background

Achievement First (AF) is a non-profit charter school network serving more than 15,000 students across 41 schools in Connecticut, New York, and Rhode Island. As our network evolves, our digital presence must effectively communicate our mission, advance our organizational goals, empower local school communities, and streamline stakeholder engagement.

AF is issuing this Request for Proposals (RFP) to select a qualified website strategy, design, and development partner to architect, redesign, rebuild, and migrate our primary digital footprint (currently hosted on WordPress at achievementfirst.org). In tandem with this web initiative, AF is finalizing an organization-wide visual brand refresh; the new digital platform will serve as the premier public presentation of our updated brand identity.

This initiative extends beyond a routine template redesign or standalone marketing site. AF requires a unified, flexible web ecosystem structured across three interdependent tiers:

  • Network Website: Representing Achievement First as a unified overarching organization.
  • Regional Hubs: Providing localized information, news, and regulatory resources for state and municipal contexts.
  • School Websites (41 Connected Micro Sites): Comprehensive pages for each school location within a single governed system, designed to adapt easily as schools open, expand, or relocate.

2. Context & Opportunity

Over years of rapid network growth, AF’s current website architecture has become fragmented. Prospective and current families encounter friction when identifying local schools or navigating enrollment pathways. Prospective educators experience challenges when applying for jobs. Donors and board members should be able to find the information they need and see clear opportunities to give and advocate for AF. Crucial school-level facts are often incomplete, while duplicated network-wide content creates operational overhead and risks content obsolescence.

The redesigned platform must deliver an exceptional user experience while introducing a sustainable content management model. Non-technical network and school staff must be empowered to publish routine updates easily within defined central guardrails for brand integrity, accessibility compliance, security, and performance.

3. Project Objectives

  • User Experience: Deliver an intuitive, mobile-first design allowing key users to complete priority tasks rapidly.
  • Brand Cohesion: Establish a recognizable AF network brand experience while honoring distinct regional and local school identities.
  • Robust Local Sites: Provide each of the 41 schools with a connected robust, functional site rather than a basic landing page.
  • Streamlined Publishing: Enable intuitive content management for non-technical users across network, regional, and school levels.
  • Governance & Workflows: Implement role-based permissions, editorial review workflows, content expiration rules, and structured approval paths.
  • Single-Source Content: Utilize structured data models so shared school and regional attributes are updated once and rendered consistently across the ecosystem.
  • Strategic Goals Support: Drive measurable conversions for student enrollment, staff recruitment, family involvement, philanthropy, and advocacy, including personalized donor engagement paths tailored to interest areas and giving levels.
  • Compliance & Standards: Exceed industry benchmarks for WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility, multilingual localization, privacy, security, page speed, SEO, and Answer Engine Optimization (AEO).
  • Scalability: Build a technical architecture that expands seamlessly as new schools, integrations, or custom tools are introduced.

4. Priority Audiences

Key audiences include prospective and enrolled families, prospective and current staff, alumni, philanthropic partners, board members, community advocates, public school authorizers, and news media. Discovery will focus on optimizing user journeys for top-priority interactions rather than treating all pages and audiences equally.

The end-user experience must be heavily optimized for mobile devices, low-bandwidth connections, assistive technology devices, and multilingual users (primarily English and Spanish speakers).

5. Detailed Scope of Work

The selected vendor will lead eight primary workstreams. Vendors should outline their proposed methodology, recommended technical stack, key assumptions, trade-offs, and explicit exclusions for each stream.

5.1 Discovery, Strategy, & User Experience

  • Facilitate stakeholder alignment on goals, KPIs, audience prioritization, governance, and risk mitigation.
  • Perform comprehensive technical, content, SEO, accessibility, and analytics audits of the existing web footprint.
  • Conduct user research with key demographics including multilingual families, staff, and mobile users.
  • Develop information architecture, user flows, navigation structures, and search strategies.
  • Produce and usability-test interactive prototypes and responsive wireframes prior to front-end engineering.

5.2 Visual Design & Brand System

  • Translate AF’s refreshed visual brand guidelines into an engaging, modern, and accessible digital design system.
  • Create responsive page designs across mobile, tablet, and desktop breakpoints for all tier templates.
  • Build a component-based design library enabling modular page creation without breaking layout rules.
  • Partner with AF’s internal design team and hand off fully documented Figma or equivalent design assets.

5.3 Multi-Tier Architecture & Ecosystem Structure

The architecture must seamlessly support three connected tiers while maintaining central governance and eliminating redundant data entry:

 

Ecosystem Tier Scope & Functional Capabilities
Network Level Overall mission, educational model, organizational stories, network leadership and boards, careers/recruitment, giving/philanthropy, press releases, and network-wide compliance resources.
Regional Hubs Region-specific directory of schools, state and city enrollment guidelines, regional leadership, news, community partnerships, public notices, localized events, and targeted emergency alerts.
41 School Pages Dedicated pages for each school featuring local navigation, school leadership, academic programming, enrollment steps, school-level volunteer sign-up and management workflows, calendars, family portals, local news, and school geography and contact information.

The platform must allow AF administrators to provision, reconfigure, or archive school sites effortlessly as the network evolves, without requiring software developer intervention.

5.4 Content Strategy, Migration, & Governance

  • Audit legacy web content, documents, media assets, active forms, and URL redirect patterns.
  • Develop a content migration strategy identifying content for retention, rewriting, consolidation, or archiving.
  • Define content taxonomies, structured fields, editorial ownership models, and review intervals.
  • Execute migration and QA for all approved network content, regional hubs, and all 41 school websites.
  • Configure granular role-based permissions (central admin, regional editor, school publisher) with approval workflows.
  • Enable cascading content distribution (e.g., syndicate a network update to all schools or specific regions).
  • Implement an organized document library for required school policies, forms, and regulatory disclosures.

5.5 Core Functionality & Technical Integrations

  • Find a School & Volunteer Portal: Interactive map and list interface with geolocation, grade-level filtering, school tour videos embedded, direct enrollment routing, and localized volunteer sign-up capabilities across individual school sites.
  • Site Search: Indexed global and scoped search across pages, documents, news, and school profiles with facet filters.
  • Forms & System Integration: Embed web forms connected to key third-party platforms (SchoolMint, HubSpot, Greenhouse, etc.).
  • Alert Banners: Targeted notification banners for emergency closures or notices at network, regional, or school levels.
  • Enhanced Funder & Donor Experience: Sophisticated, tiered donor pathways going beyond a single landing page to present customized content, impact stories, high-impact video storytelling capabilities, digital Annual Report (AR) vision integration, and engagement opportunities based on donor interest areas and giving levels.
  • Technical SEO & Answer Engine Optimization (AEO): Structured schema markup, XML sitemaps, semantic tags, automated redirect handling, and AEO strategies to optimize content discovery across AI search engines and conversational assistants.
  • Analytics & Tracking: Google Tag Manager & GA4 setup supporting rollup and property-level event/conversion reporting, including custom GA4 reports and event tracking dashboards tailored to core functional areas such as student enrollment and talent recruitment.

Current and anticipated integrations include SchoolMint/HubSpot (Enrollment CRM), Greenhouse (Careers), Salesforce & Fundraise Up (Fundraising), ParentSquare, Infinite Campus, Google Maps API, and Google Analytics. Vendors should detail experience integrating these or similar systems.

5.6 Accessibility, Localization, & Web Performance

  • WCAG 2.2 AA Compliance: Full adherence to WCAG 2.2 Level AA standards verified via automated tools and manual screen-reader/keyboard testing.
  • Multilingual Architecture: Seamless multi-language framework supporting human translations, intuitive language selectors, and localized metadata.
  • Document Accessibility: Strategy for handling non-compliant legacy PDFs/documents, identifying core scope vs. optional remediation.
  • Core Web Vitals Performance: Target mobile metrics at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1.
  • Lighthouse Benchmarks: Target scores of 90+ across Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, and SEO for key page templates.

5.7 Platform, Architecture, & Security

  • CMS Selection: Recommend a modern, secure CMS (e.g., Enterprise WordPress, WordPress Multisite, Drupal, or Headless solution) suitable for non-profit scale.
  • Editing Experience: Structured block-based authoring experience preventing accidental layout breaking or accessibility non-compliance.
  • Security Protocols: Role-based access controls, SSO/MFA/SCIM capability, SSL, automated backups (vendor must specify backup frequency, retention period, and storage location [on-platform vs. offsite/geo-redundant]; minimum requirements include daily automated backups across all 41 school sites plus network and regional tiers, 30-day retention minimum, Recovery Point Objective [RPO] of 24 hours or less, and Recovery Time Objective [RTO] of 8 hours or less for full platform restore; vendor must describe restore process and confirm testing), vulnerability scanning, and DDoS protection.
  • Environments & Deployment: Isolated Dev, Staging, and Production environments with Git-based CI/CD deployment workflows.
  • Full Ownership: AF retains 100% ownership of code repositories, design assets, content, data, domain registrations, and credentials.
  • Vendor Independence: Avoid proprietary lock-in; transparently disclose all licensing fees, external plugins, and SaaS dependencies.

5.8 Training, Launch, & Post-Launch Warranty

  • Role-Based Training: Provide tailored live training and recorded video documentation for central admins, regional editors, and school publishers.
  • Migration Transition: Coordinate seamless handoff and DNS cutover with AF’s internal IT team and incumbent web host.
  • Comprehensive QA: Execute multi-browser/device testing, broken-link verification, 301 redirect validation, and accessibility audits prior to launch.
  • Stabilization & Warranty: Provide a post-launch stabilization period and minimum 90-day bug-fix warranty.
  • Ongoing SLA Options: Separately priced annual maintenance, hosting, monitoring, and support packages with clear SLAs. SLA pricing must itemize backup/DR and vulnerability management as line items, not bundle them into a general “hosting and support” fee, so AF can compare vendors on these specifically.

6. Critical Proposal Requirements

Proposals will be evaluated on the agency’s ability to meet the following core requirements:

  1. Demonstrated ability to deploy a unified, multi-tier ecosystem (Network, Regional, 41 School Pages).
  2. Intuitive CMS publishing experience tailored for non-technical content managers.
  3. Single-source data modeling for recurring regional and school attributes.
  4. Centralized content syndication capabilities with localized editing controls.
  5. Mobile-optimized user journeys featuring a high-performing “Find a School” search experience.
  6. Embedded WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standard compliance across design and code.
  7. Concrete content migration plan covering all network, regional, and school pages.
  8. Transparent technology dependencies and full IP ownership transfer to AF.
  9. Clear pricing schedule aligning with AF’s non-profit budget parameters.

7. Required Deliverables

The engagement deliverables shall include, at minimum:

  • Discovery summary report, user research findings, and technical audit documentation.
  • Approved information architecture, sitemaps, wireframes, and interactive UX prototypes.
  • Digital brand strategy, UI design system, component library, and Figma design files.
  • Fully configured CMS hosting the network site, regional hubs, and 41 school websites.
  • Custom-developed components (Find a School tool, faceted search, alert system, integrations).
  • Completed content migration, formatting, QA, and automated 301 URL redirects.
  • Accessibility compliance verification and Web Vitals performance benchmark reports.
  • Admin and editor training sessions, video tutorials, and system documentation.
  • Production deployment, post-launch stabilization support, and warranty plan.

8. Vendor Qualifications & Expertise

Eligible agencies must demonstrate extensive experience in executing complex digital projects. Key qualifications include:

  • Proven track record of designing, building, and launching large-scale web platforms.
  • Three relevant case studies demonstrating multi-site, federated, or enterprise CMS implementations.
  • In-house expertise across digital strategy, UX/UI, accessibility compliance, and engineering.
  • Experience working with non-profits, public K-12 education, or public sector clients.
  • Strong project management methodologies and executive account governance.
  • Adequate insurance coverage (General Liability, Cyber Liability, Errors & Omissions).

Subcontractors, if proposed, must be explicitly declared alongside details regarding their specific roles, geographic location, and quality assurance oversight.

9. Proposal Format & Submissions

Vendors must organize proposals strictly in the following order to facilitate comparative evaluation:

Proposal Section Required Content & Details
1. Executive Summary Understanding of project goals, proposed strategic approach, key differentiators, and major project risks/assumptions.
2. Agency Profile & Team Agency overview, team staffing structure, key personnel bios, locations, executive sponsor, PM methodology, and staffing continuity plan.
3. Case Studies & References Three relevant case studies (including multi-site implementations) with measurable outcomes, live URLs, and client references.
4. Technical Solution Proposed CMS platform, multi-tier architecture, publishing/editing workflows, accessibility approach, performance strategies, and security measures.
5. Work Plan & Schedule Phased implementation methodology, timeline, review milestones, client dependencies, and targeted launch date recommendation.
6. Pricing & TCO Fixed-fee implementation breakdown by phase, optional services, hourly rates, recurring licensing/hosting fees, and 5-year Total Cost of Ownership.
7. Terms & Compliance Security practices, data protection policies, insurance verification, IP ownership confirmation, and requested contract exceptions.

10. Budgetary Framework

AF anticipates an implementation budget covering discovery, strategy, UX/UI design, front- and back-end development, migration, initial content entry, QA, training, and launch. Ongoing hosting, SLA support, maintenance, software licenses, and third-party tools should be itemized separately.
