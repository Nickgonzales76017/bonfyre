# Client sales and working previews

Current instructions from Nick supersede the older outreach drafts.

## Work sequence

Start from a specific retained client request and inspect the existing business. Identify the work the client wants covered, their current tools, useful public material and the missing facts that affect the offer. Reuse the existing Project, CMS, WORK, finance, provider and presentation owners. Do not create another lead database or a separate demo application framework.

Use Google Stitch to design the useful tool or flow around that client's work. Google Stitch is the external design service at stitch.withgoogle.com; BonfyreStitch is a different native command. Use the actual connected Stitch service and retain its screen/project reference. Never label a manually written page or a prepared prompt as a Stitch result.

A design must include the useful interaction, not merely a dashboard illustration. Map the design back into existing frontend components and working operations. Connect useful source data, test the main action, reload state, handle an invalid input and check the exact shared route in a fresh desktop and mobile browser. A CSV may be a supporting export; it is not the product. An API response or HTTP 200 does not establish that the client can finish the task.

Keep the client's language and visual identity prominent. Use restrained supplier branding. Skip generic AI hero copy, architecture diagrams, internal command names and giant headers. When account access is unavailable, prepare the exact brief and existing implementation mapping; say that design generation is pending rather than inventing a substitute result.

## Copy editing

For opening messages, marketplace proposals and follow-ups, read [references/outreach-patterns.md](references/outreach-patterns.md). Choose the pattern after reading the full request; the examples are starting points, not text to broadcast.

Read ../humanizer/SKILL.md and use embedded mode. Return only the finished customer copy. Use a second editing pass with the source request and commercial objective in view. Preserve the offer, relevant facts and the client's terminology. Cut method narration, generic praise, exaggerated significance, staged contrasts, disclaimers about unrelated work, lab statistics and decorative formatting. This is sales writing: retain a useful benefit and a clear invitation. Do not flatten it into a technical report or remove the commercial offer merely because it is persuasive.

The first note should normally fit on a phone screen: a natural subject, one client-specific reason for writing, a useful service or working preview, and one easy reply. Keep longer content only when the posting specifically asks for answers or application details. Add no unsolicited attachments by default. Link only to a tested, directly relevant working route. Do not mention nonexistent previews or repeat earlier messages to the same prospect.

Initial phone and video meetings are free. Do not offer an in-person visit in mass outreach. Local visits can be discussed individually after interest. Prices are for the delivered work or ongoing coverage, never the introductory meeting. Prepare two or three coherent price/scope options internally rather than attach arbitrary fees to a row count.

Nick's 2026-10-07 direction is to sell fully remote delivery using technology, existing infrastructure and suitable external services. His later correction is to earn interest before discussing pricing. Lead with the buyer's specific problem, a useful result or working example, and one easy reply. Use business and market research to choose the offer and its positioning. Do not lead with invented fixed prices, arbitrary batch sizes or cheap trial packages. Preserve buyer-stated rates as source evidence; they are not our quote. After interest, inspect the work and discuss scope, urgency, volume, acceptance and commercial terms. Keep price comparisons and negotiating options internal until that conversation; include their assumptions instead of treating an old estimate as a standing price.

Do not ask Nick to supply weekly staffing hours or make an hourly commitment the condition for pursuing a prospect. In-person meetings may help demonstrate the work; they are not the service model. Preserve a posting's actual hours and location requirements as source evidence, and describe the remote offer as a proposed change of scope that the buyer must accept. For physical items, establish who supplies photos, measurements or scans. Do not promise an unconfirmed local operator, specialist or account access. Show the relevant working interaction when available and state what remains to be built internally.

Work as a Bonfyre/Aurekai user throughout: combine the live site, native commands or MCP operations, existing infrastructure and external services. Use the existing owners to research, prepare, execute and retain results. When that work exposes a missing capability or broken path, repair or extend its owner and retry the same real task. One-off shell scripts must not become the operating workflow or a separate client database.

Nick requested 15 distinct, researched drafts with an execution approach and pricing choices for his final approval. Preserve drafts in the existing outbound_message owner. Do not dispatch this batch until he approves it. Keep sent history separate so revision work does not resend an existing message.
