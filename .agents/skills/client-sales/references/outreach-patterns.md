# Outreach patterns

These original examples adapt the useful structure of the sources below to Nick's remote delivery model. Replace bracketed material with verified details. Choose one next step; a reply can be easier than booking a meeting. Use the buyer's stated application channel. A template does not authorize sending.

## Read the whole request

Before preparing a first contact, check existing project records, sent mail, replies and drafts for the organization and its contact domain. Grant applications count too. An existing application should lead to a thread-aware follow-up or revision, with its original scope preserved. Record the thread and draft identifiers so final sending cannot accidentally create a second application. A draft is not evidence of contact.

Search all known contacts at the organization, not just the first thread found. A recent sent reply in another thread can supersede older drafts that still exist. Record that relationship in the review and prepare the next useful deliverable; do not send another follow-up merely because an older draft is unsent.

Before drafting, record the desired result, existing stack, application instructions, location or coverage requirements, buyer-stated budget, and evidence we can show. A job-board headline may hide a required visit, a closed application, a full-project quote, or an award contingency. Read amendments and Q&A for procurements. Treat other applicants' claims as their claims, not our portfolio or the buyer's requirements.

Nick wants interest before pricing. Opening emails should not introduce a preset fee. If a marketplace or formal bid requires a price, record that requirement and prepare a scoped estimate separately; an interest note alone is not a complete application. Do not invent a nominal bid to get through a form.

## Useful follow-up

Hi [name],

Since my note about [task], [a real new finding or completed improvement]. [Explain why it matters to their request and link to the evidence if useful.]

Is [specific outcome] still something you need help with?

Nick

Use only after checking sent history and replies. Do not claim they opened a message, imply an earlier conversation, or manufacture urgency. Stop when they decline.

## Formal procurement or subcontract

Build the response around the solicitation's requirements and evaluation criteria. Include the requested qualifications, references, scope, commercial terms and submission format. A short interest email cannot replace a compliant proposal. Where a prime needs delivery support, identify the proposed responsibility and actual evidence without claiming a partnership already exists. Preserve award contingencies and response commitments; investigate suitable specialists before promising their availability.

## Grant application follow-up

Reply in the existing application thread. Name the application and date, give one useful development or a narrower deliverable that still serves the original scope, and ask one question the funder can answer. Recheck the current program rules and the public evidence links. Keep the grant's published range separate from a requested budget. Do not renew historical timing promises, claim eligibility or accept funding conditions on Nick's behalf.
