A working application used to take a great deal of engineering time to produce. That hasn't vanished, but AI has changed how much a small team can build. What hasn't changed nearly as quickly is everything around the application: who needs it, how their company works, where the data lives, what they're willing to pay, and who keeps the result running six months later.

That tension kept coming up in our Aurekai planning conversations. We could imagine an agent building a new product over a weekend, then spending weeks trying to connect it to accounts, policies, customers, support, invoices and the rest of a business. Producing the code was increasingly the easiest part.

## The unit of work is getting bigger

Aurekai is our attempt to work on the whole business at once. An independent store might need a website, inventory, customer accounts, invoices and staff tools. A publisher might need profiles, recordings, subscriptions, distribution and community pages. They can look very different to customers while sharing much of the same underlying engineering.

That is why we talk about engineering as capital. When we build a useful catalogue tool, it can serve more than one retailer. A source-library component can help a publisher, a research workspace or a creator. An identity and permissions system can support both of them. The next opportunity can begin with something substantial already in place.

## A small team still needs deep expertise

AI helps with unfamiliar code, but operating a business still requires judgment about tax, finance, privacy, fulfillment, labor, customer relationships and service. We don't want those disciplines replaced by a generic prompt. We want applications and tools that make the expertise available at the point where it's needed.

Bonfÿre is the native platform we use to organize that work: Matrix for people and sources, WorkGraph for tasks and capabilities, application grammars for CRM and accounting, model tools for analysis, and public web systems where people interact with the results.

The ambition is a company that gets better at building and operating companies. Its public products are where people encounter the work. The shared software is what lets the work accumulate.
