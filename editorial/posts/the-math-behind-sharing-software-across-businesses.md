A small retailer may need a website, support, inventory, invoices and a modest amount of marketing. Building those systems independently for every retailer is expensive, even when an AI agent can generate much of the code. Operating and integrating them still consumes time.

Aurekai's business model depends on the economics of sharing that work across distinct businesses.

## A simple example

Suppose the fixed software setup for one independent business costs $12,000. Five businesses buying five separate implementations would spend $60,000 before considering their ongoing costs.

Now imagine a shared platform costs $16,000 to establish and each customer-specific implementation costs another $3,000. Five businesses would need $16,000 + 5 × $3,000 = **$31,000**. The difference is $29,000 in this illustrative model.

The calculation isn't a prediction. It isolates an economy of scope: one investment supports several outputs.

## What cannot be shared safely

Each business still needs its own customer relationships, access permissions, prices, brand and legitimate data ownership. Some industries have unique compliance or operational requirements. Over-sharing creates its own costs through complexity, security risk and a generic interface that nobody likes.

The true condition is that **shared fixed cost + adaptation + coordination < independent cost**. As the portfolio grows, coordination may stop being cheap. That is why technical ownership and account separation matter as much as reuse.

The [U.S. Chamber's 2026 technology report](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/2026-empowering-small-business-report) describes how many platforms small businesses already depend on. Aurekai's opportunity is to connect and improve them while preserving what makes each enterprise distinct.

A good shared platform lowers the cost of operating a small business without making all businesses look or behave alike.
