A property marketplace has attractive structured material: addresses, rooms, prices, photographs, history and descriptions. That makes it tempting to build a new product by collecting listings and arranging them differently.

The difficult part is not reading the fields. It is understanding their source, permitted uses, freshness and relationship to the real estate professionals responsible for the information.

## Standards solve one problem

The [RESO Data Dictionary](https://dd.reso.org/) defines common resources and fields for exchanging real estate data. That can make integrations between participating systems easier and reduce the number of incompatible local formats.

But a common field name doesn't grant a licence to reuse listing photographs or redistribute all market data. Access agreements, copyright, provider contracts and current rules still determine what a product may do.

## A better product than a copied portal

Aurekai has explored property and housing services alongside its account applications. A useful experience might help a buyer compare the costs of repairs, locate local services, organize inspection documents or connect a property decision to an actual project budget.

That work can use permitted listings, official public records and customer-supplied documents while retaining the different sources and their restrictions. A buyer could see a property estimate, a contractor's quote and an insurance document together without presenting them as the same kind of fact.

The opportunity is to make property decisions easier to complete. The data standard is infrastructure for that process; it is not permission to scrape a market without limits.
