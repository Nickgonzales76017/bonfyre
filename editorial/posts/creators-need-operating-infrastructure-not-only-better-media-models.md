The creator economy produces an enormous amount of media, but the business around a creator can remain fragmented. One app stores recordings, another edits clips, a third manages followers and a spreadsheet tracks sponsorship obligations.

The [IAB's 2026 CreatorFronts discussion](https://www.iab.com/blog/2026-iab-creatorfronts-event-recap/) describes significant projected U.S. advertising spend and continuing work to make the market easier to operate.

## A publication is an organization of sources

A writer, podcaster or educator may have a large back catalogue that remains valuable years after a post was first released. Recording files, transcripts, contributors, permissions and related products need stable identities and a sensible place to live.

Aurekai Live and Life approach that need from different sides. Live focuses on media and channels. Life gives people, organizations and communities recognizable public homes. Source Library connects the original material to the places it is used.

## The commercial work also matters

A creator may need a catalogue, subscription, sponsorship agreement, invoice or customer relationship. That begins to look like ordinary business software, adapted to the rhythms of publishing.

A practical product could connect a recorded interview to edited clips, a public article, a member collection and an offer for a related service. The creator should remain able to see which material is used where and who has permission to change it.

Better speech and video models will help produce more material. We are interested in the infrastructure that makes the body of work useful and the creator's business sustainable.
